Nothing Wrong With the Way You're Made

Three generations of speech class - and day one of talking about it.

Kevin Copeland - KEVIN COPELAND LLC

November 12 2026

I took speech as a kid.

I remember the feeling more than the classes - having something in my head and watching it come out of my mouth wrong. I grew up being a person who "doesn't open up a lot," and I told myself that was just how I was built. It took my son's diagnosis paperwork for me to start asking whether it was a personality or a survival strategy.

Then this year I learned two things in the same season: my brother took speech too. And so did my mom.

Three generations. It never came up. Not once. I still don't fully know why - maybe shame, maybe just a family that keeps moving. But you'd be surprised what you don't know about your own family until you start asking certain questions.

Meanwhile, my son is in speech right now. It's going great - and I don't say that lightly, because nothing about this road has been light. Autism. ADHD. Developmental delay. He's a couple of years behind where the chart says he should be, and not having a steady teacher hasn't helped. We're starting ABA therapy. There's an alphabet soup of acronyms I can now spell in my sleep, and a stack of paperwork on my desk that would scare a paralegal.

Here's what stops me cold, though. My son has help because somebody asked questions. He has speech because we asked. He has a plan because we pushed for one. My mom and my brother navigated the same wires by themselves, in a generation where nobody talked about any of this. And so did I - for a long time.

I believe I have ADHD too. I've believed it for a while. I'm the dad sitting in the IEP meetings nodding along to descriptions of my son's brain and quietly recognizing my own reflection. The apple didn't just fall close to the tree. The apple is still in the tree.

So this is day one of me talking. Not because I'm suddenly comfortable - I recorded my first video sitting in a parked car, and comfort had nothing to do with it. I'm doing it because I learned something this year that I think every parent in this world needs to hear:

Speaking about yourself gives other people permission to speak about themselves.

When I tell my story, my family's story changes. When my son sees me working on my words - writing them, recording them, fumbling them and trying again - he's not learning that adults have it all together. He's learning that you're allowed to be exactly who you are and still grow. That's the lesson I want him to have years before I got it.

If you're the parent who doesn't open up a lot - the one at the meeting with the tight jaw, the one who has read everything and said nothing - I'm not going to tell you to change who you are. There's nothing wrong with the way you're made. There's nothing wrong with the way you operate. But there's a difference between how you're made and what you're carrying alone. The first one is yours to keep. The second one is negotiable.

Ask your family some questions. You'll be surprised what you don't know.

And if you've been waiting for a sign to start saying things out loud - this is it. I'm a dad in a parked car, talking to his phone. Day one. If I can start, so can you.

- Kevin
